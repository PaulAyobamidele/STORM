\section{What was tested}
% =====================================================================

The subject is \textbf{Spliit}, an open-source expense-sharing web application
(Next.js~16, tRPC~11, Prisma~6, PostgreSQL~17). Unlike a local-first
application, every write crosses a network to a server and a database, and
every read of a derived view (totals, activity log) is served through a client
cache. The application was run from its own Docker composition and driven
through a fault proxy that can refuse its API calls.

A formal specification (LNT, 486 states), a System Interface describing how each
abstract gate is realised in the browser, and an interpretation of the abstract
domain are composed (3\,719 states, 4\,497 transitions) and passed to
CADP/TESTOR, which generates controllable test cases. A concretization layer walks
each test case against the running application through a headless browser,
applies disruptions out of band, and returns an \textbf{ioco} verdict.

Eleven test purposes were generated, yielding \textbf{16 test cases}. Twelve
walks are reported: the eleven canonical cases and two \emph{fault-first}
variants, for reasons Section~\ref{sec:stale} explains.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The specification states obligations, not the application's
habits.} Before any run, the behaviour of the application was studied from its
source and seven obligations were written into the specification header --- the
behaviour a careful user is entitled to, in the spirit of the reporting and
consistency duties of the EU AI Act, not the behaviour the source was seen to
have:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llp{7.2cm}@{}}
\toprule
 & Obligation & Observable the specification demands \\
\midrule
\obl{1} & a rejected save is reported & \gate{REJECT\_SHOWN} \\
\obl{2} & the log covers every row a write made & \gate{LOGGED}, also for weekly copies \\
\obl{3} & the total shown is the committed one & the band after a write, cache warm or not \\
\obl{4} & a failed save is reported & \gate{WRITE\_ERROR\_SHOWN} \\
\obl{5} & the log never claims a write that failed & \gate{UNLOGGED} after a rollback \\
\obl{6} & a failed rate lookup is reported & \gate{RATE\_ERROR\_SHOWN} \\
\obl{7} & a corrupt record is flagged when opened & \gate{DB\_CORRUPT\_DETECTED} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Written this way, the nominal path itself can fail. The source study
forecast, for each disruption, whether the application would meet its
obligation; the forecasts were recorded in the disruption mapping before the
first run and are compared with the outcome in Section~\ref{sec:summary}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Conformance is relative to the specification.} A \textsc{fail}
means the application produced an output the specification forbids after a given
trace --- including \emph{quiescence}, the absence of an output the
specification requires. Whether the specification is itself a faithful account
of what should be required is treated in Section~\ref{sec:threats}.

\noindent\textbf{\textsc{pass}} --- the test case reached its accept state, every
output observed on the way being one the specification permits. For an output
that carries values (\gate{CONFIRM (status, band, log, id)}) this means the
concrete readings --- the total on the Stats tab, the text of the activity list
--- were interpreted into an abstract label and that label is the one the case
offers. A walk that reaches the accept state without that interpretation proves
nothing; one such walk occurred before the interpreting oracle existed and is
not counted.

\noindent\textbf{\textsc{fail}} --- an output outside the set the specification
permits after the trace. Two forms occurred: the application produced a value the
case does not offer (a total that had not moved), and the application produced
nothing within the observation window at a state where the case offers no
$\delta$.

\noindent\textbf{\textsc{inconclusive}} --- the application produced nothing at a
state where the case offers $\delta$. None occurred.

\noindent\textbf{\textsc{unexecutable}} --- not a verdict. The disruption could
not be confirmed in place, or the apparatus failed, so the walk did not test what
the case names. None occurred in the batch reported here; Section~\ref{sec:batch}
records a batch that was discarded on these grounds.

\section{The nominal campaign}
% =====================================================================

The \gate{nominal} purpose exercises the application with no disruption: create
a group, add one expense of 40.00 paid by one participant, read the group total
on the Stats tab, read the activity log. It is the \textbf{control}: it
establishes that the selectors, the typing and clicking, and the interpretation
of the two readings are right, so that a verdict under disruption can be
attributed to the disruption.

\section{The disruptions}
% =====================================================================

Seven disruptions were injected out of band --- through the fault proxy, SQL
against the application's database, the container runtime, or the host's name
resolution --- never through the user interface. Three more are carried as
parameters of the write rather than injected: the tester performs them as a user
would. Every injected disruption was verified to \emph{bite} (a probe confirmed
its effect independently of the application) before the walk continued, and to
\emph{let go} after it.

\subsection{\gate{input\_invalid}, \gate{ue\_kill}, \gate{app\_extapi\_fail} --- the passes}

With amount~0 the form shows \emph{``The amount must not be zero.''} before
anything is sent; the case observes \gate{REJECT\_SHOWN}, the tester cancels,
and the read-back interprets \texttt{\$0.00} and the empty-list sentence
\emph{``There is not yet any activity in your group.''} as \gate{S0} and
\gate{UNLOGGED} --- the one \gate{CONFIRM} the case offers. \obl{1} is met for
the client-side check.

Leaving the page immediately after Create and returning, the tester found the
expense and its log entry both present: the request had completed after the
browser left. The case offers both consistent outcomes (whole, or nothing) and
observed the first.

With \texttt{api.frankfurter.app} resolved to the loopback address, choosing a
foreign currency produced \emph{``Oops, we could not get the most recent rates.
Enter a custom rate below.''}; the case observes \gate{RATE\_ERROR\_SHOWN}, the
write then commits, and the read-back agrees. \obl{6} is met.

\subsection{\gate{app\_unavailable}, \gate{db\_abort}, \gate{infra\_db\_down} --- \obl{4}, three layers, one silence}
\label{sec:silent}

\paragraph{The setup.}
Three different things are broken, each aimed at the same obligation --- a save
that fails is reported to the user: the API refused at the proxy, the insert
aborted inside the database, the database unreachable for 25\,s. Network,
database engine, infrastructure: three independent paths to one requirement.

\paragraph{What the tester did.}
Filled the form and pressed Create, then waited for the form to say something.

\paragraph{What the specification requires.}
After any of the three, exactly one output is permitted, \gate{WRITE\_ERROR\_SHOWN},
realised as any error text on the form, any element with an alert role, or any
toast. The generated case offers no $\delta$ at that state.

\paragraph{What the application did.}
Nothing, in all three cases, for the length of the observation window: the
Create button re-enabled and the form stayed. Three \textsc{fail}s by quiescence.
The source explains the silence: no error boundary on the route, the mutation
awaited without a handler, and the asynchronous button swallowing its rejection.

\paragraph{The database tells the three apart.}
Under \gate{APP\_UNAVAILABLE} nothing reached the server: no expense, no log row.
Under \gate{DB\_ABORT} the expense was refused \emph{and the log row was written
anyway}: the application logs before it writes, outside any transaction
(Section~\ref{sec:finding}). Under \gate{INFRA\_DB\_DOWN} the request hung while
the database was paused and \textbf{completed after it returned}: the expense and
its log row both exist. The obligation violated there is ``the user is told'',
not ``the write is kept''; a reader should know the write was delayed, not lost.

\subsection{\gate{input\_overlimit} --- \obl{1}, the server's silent no}

The browser validates the amount in major units and the server in minor units,
so 20\,000\,000 passes the form and is refused by the server. The specification
requires \gate{REJECT\_SHOWN}; the form showed nothing and stayed put. The
database holds neither an expense nor a log row: the server refused before
logging, so this silence, unlike \gate{DB\_ABORT}'s, leaves no trace. A user who
typed a large amount is told nothing and is left with an unsaved form.

\subsection{\gate{app\_cache\_stale} --- \obl{3}, a view that does not follow the write}
\label{sec:stale}

\paragraph{The setup.}
Nothing is broken. The Stats tab is opened once before the expense is written.

\paragraph{What the specification requires.}
After a committed write of one 40.00 expense the total must read one rung:
\gate{CONFIRM (COMMITTED, S1, LOGGED, exp\_c)}.

\paragraph{What the application did.}
The Stats tab read \texttt{\$0.00}. The write had committed (one expense, one
log row in the database); the view served a cached total. The source gives the
mechanism: the query client keeps results fresh for 30\,s, and the create form
invalidates only the expense list, never the totals or the activity log. Both
derived views therefore stay stale for up to 30\,s after any write that follows
a read.

\paragraph{The canonical case never reached its disruption.}
The canonical \gate{app\_cache\_stale} case begins with three ordinary writes and
applies the priming visit only before the fourth. It failed at the
\emph{second} write: the System Interface reads Stats back after every write,
that read warms the cache, and the next write's read-back returned the previous
total (\texttt{\$40.00} where the case offers only \gate{S2}). The same holds
for the canonical \gate{app\_extapi\_fail} case and for variants 1--3 of both:
any case with two writes within 30\,s fails on \obl{3} before its disruption.
Variant~4 of each, which applies the disruption first, is the one reported.

\medskip
\noindent\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.95\textwidth}
The stale total is not a fault the tester injects. It is the application's
default after any read, and it shows on the nominal shape of the specification
as soon as a second write follows a first within the cache window.
\end{minipage}}

\subsection{\gate{weekly} --- \obl{2}, a row the log does not know}

An expense dated eight days back with weekly recurrence is written. On the next
read the server materialises the copy that has fallen due: the database holds
two expenses (dated the 20th and the 27th), the Stats tab reads \texttt{\$80.00}
(\gate{S2}, both counted), and the activity log names the write \textbf{once}.
The case waits for a second entry naming it (\gate{EL\_ACTIVITY\_COPY}) and
offers no $\delta$; none appeared. The materialiser runs inside a transaction on
every read and writes no activity row. A row exists, is counted, and is
unaccounted for.

\subsection{\gate{db\_corrupt} --- \obl{7}, a row that opens as if whole}

After one committed write (the first \gate{CONFIRM} agreed: \gate{S1},
\gate{LOGGED}), the expense's split rows are deleted by SQL; the probe confirmed
zero remained. The tester opened the row. The specification requires
\gate{DB\_CORRUPT\_DETECTED}, realised as any error text or alert on the edit
page; the page rendered the form and said nothing. A \textsc{fail} by
quiescence: the record is shown for editing with its payment split gone.

\section{A batch that was discarded}
\label{sec:batch}
% =====================================================================

The first full batch is not reported. Its \gate{db\_abort} walk installed its
trigger and the framework never removed it: the walker collected the faults it
injected and restored none of them. Every insert in the five cases that followed
was aborted by a disruption those cases had not asked for --- \gate{ue\_kill}
showed the \gate{DB\_ABORT} signature, two cases stalled on the form and were
reported \textsc{unexecutable}, and \gate{infra\_db\_down}'s own pause had been
backgrounded by a shell precedence slip and never confirmed, yet a \textsc{fail}
was recorded. A sixth, \gate{input\_invalid}, failed because the observable for
``no activity'' was the first list entry, which an empty list does not have.

All of these were the tester's. The framework now restores every injected
disruption after every walk and re-probes it; discards any verdict whose
disruption was not confirmed; judges a missing observable by the case rather than
by which primitive missed it; and the activity observable reads the list's text,
which exists whether or not the list is empty. The batch of
Section~\ref{sec:results} was then run from a stack verified clean (trigger
count~0, database unpaused, proxy without faults), and ended with the trigger
count still~0.

\section{A finding the verdicts could not express}
\label{sec:finding}
% =====================================================================

Under \gate{DB\_ABORT} the walk ends at \obl{4}: the form said nothing, the case
offers no $\delta$, \textsc{fail}. It never reaches the \gate{CONFIRM} at which
\obl{5} --- the log never claims a write that failed --- would be judged. The
database reached it anyway. The groups created by the reported batch, newest
first, with their expense and activity-row counts:

\begin{lstlisting}
 created  | expenses | activity_rows | split_rows   case
 11:21:15 |        1 |             1 |          0   db_corrupt
 11:21:13 |        2 |             2 |          0   app_cache_stale (canonical)
 11:20:47 |        1 |             1 |          0   ue_kill
 11:20:34 |        0 |             0 |          0   input_overlimit
 11:20:32 |        0 |             0 |          0   input_invalid
 11:20:18 |        1 |             1 |          0   infra_db_down
 11:20:04 |        0 |             1 |          0   db_abort
 11:19:50 |        0 |             0 |          0   app_unavailable
 11:19:49 |        1 |             1 |          0   nominal
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent The \gate{db\_abort} group holds an activity row --- \emph{``Expense
``Dinner C'' created by Someone.''} --- for an expense that does not exist. The
source shows why: every logging write puts the activity row \emph{before} the
domain write, outside any transaction. A user opening the activity tab is told an
expense was created that the group does not contain.

No \textbf{ioco} verdict reports this, because the walk stopped, correctly, at
the first non-conformance. It is a finding of a query against the application's
own store, not a conformance verdict, and it is reported as such. Making it a
verdict would require the specification to let the walk continue past a missing
error report to the read-back --- that is, to weaken \obl{4} --- and that is a
choice, not a repair.

\medskip
\noindent\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.95\textwidth}
The same pattern --- an aborted write whose log entry survives --- is the one
\gate{WEEKLY} shows from the other side: a write that succeeds and leaves no log
entry. The application's log and its data are maintained separately, and the
campaign found both directions in which they can disagree.
\end{minipage}}

\section{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:threats}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{Changes made during the campaign.}
The framework was corrected while the campaign ran (Section~\ref{sec:batch}),
and two further defects of the tester's were found and fixed before the reported
batch: a walk could reach its accept state without interpreting a valued output
(the first \gate{nominal} pass, discarded), and every generated case offered
$\delta$ at every state because the specification's disruptor carried an
internal no-op branch, which the generator reads as livelock and therefore as
permitted quiescence. The branch was removed and every case regenerated: the
composition lost exactly one internal loop per state and no case changed its
shape. A second regeneration moved the \gate{UE\_KILL} gate before the click it
shared with the commit arm, for the same reason. All changes are SUT-agnostic
and were checked with self-tests; none touched a verdict's meaning after the
reported batch began.

\paragraph{The abstraction.}
The total is read in rungs of one expense, and every expense the tester writes
is 40.00 so that a rung is a row; the application's arithmetic is not under
test. The log is read as ``does the list name the row just written'', which in
a fresh group per walk is the newest entry. Whether an entry is the
\emph{newest} is not checked.

\paragraph{Windows.}
Quiescence is decided at 10\,s. The application's cache window is 30\,s and
every read-back in the campaign fell inside it; \gate{INFRA\_DB\_DOWN}'s pause
was 25\,s and the delayed write landed after the walk had stopped. A different
window would not change any verdict here, but it would change the wording of
that one.

\paragraph{Coverage not achieved.}
Variants 1--3 of \gate{app\_cache\_stale} and \gate{app\_extapi\_fail} were not
walked (Section~\ref{sec:stale}). Only one participant ever paid, no expense was
ever edited or deleted through the walk (the \gate{VIEW}/\gate{REMOVE} arms are
refused by every purpose), and reimbursements were not exercised. No
coverage measure of the specification was computed.

\paragraph{Verification of restore.}
\gate{DB\_CORRUPT} (nothing to restore in a fresh group) and
\gate{APP\_EXTAPI\_FAIL} (a hosts-file line placed and removed by hand, since the
harness cannot answer a sudo prompt) declare no restore probe; the log says so.

\paragraph{Single execution; one environment.}
One repetition per case, one stack, one headless browser, one machine. No
screenshot evidence is captured on the web executor; the evidence is the walk
log and the database.

% =====================================================================
\section{Summary}
\label{sec:summary}
% =====================================================================

\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{2pt}
  \item Twelve walks over eleven purposes: \textbf{4 \textsc{pass}, 8
        \textsc{fail}}, no \textsc{inconclusive}, no \textsc{unexecutable}.
        Every injected disruption was confirmed in place before, and confirmed
        gone after, the walk it belongs to.
  \item Every forecast written before the first run matched its outcome; the
        one left open, \gate{UE\_KILL}, came out \textsc{pass}.
  \item Six failures are \emph{silence where a report was owed}: a failed save
        at three layers (\obl{4}), a server-side rejection (\obl{1}), a corrupt
        row opened without a notice (\obl{7}), a materialised row the log never
        mentions (\obl{2}). Two are \emph{a value the specification forbids}: a
        total that did not follow the write (\obl{3}), once under the intended
        disruption and once on the nominal shape at the second write.
  \item The passes are the client-side rejection (\obl{1}), the rate-service
        failure (\obl{6}) --- the one failure the application reports in words
        --- and a consistent outcome when the user leaves mid-submit.
  \item One finding no verdict could express: an activity row for an expense
        that was never written (\obl{5} in the data), because the application
        logs before it writes and the walk stops at the first non-conformance.
  \item The tester's own first batch was discarded and the reasons recorded.
\end{itemize}
